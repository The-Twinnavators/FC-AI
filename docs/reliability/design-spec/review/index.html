Across the journey: what holds, what doesn't

Holds on every page, in all three: the heading and text families, the palette and page colour, the surface treatment (flat outlines, or the neo-brutalist outlines and hard shadows), field and button corners, the frame and its navigation. No page errors and no sideways scrolling at either width.

Inconsistencies (reported, not fixed here)

  • Product pictures change by page. Home and Catalog draw a tile with the category icon; the Product page shows initials ("OM"). Same product, two looks.
  • Heading sizes differ by page type (Bold aisle: Home 25.6px, Product 22.8px, Catalog 20.3px; Editorial: Product 45px, Catalog 32px). These follow intended roles (display heading, record title, page title; see EVAL4-PLAN.md §2); pages with the same role match. Editorial's large record title is a choice for you to accept or reject.
  • Button heights: 48px for Home's hero action, 40px for standard actions: intended roles, not a defect.
  • Two ZIP prompts on Product: the frame's "Find it near you" banner and the page's own "Where to find it" form. On a phone the banner takes most of the first screen on every page but Home. Correction proposed (§3B).
  • Save iconography: the frame's mobile bar shows Saved with a heart, while the save toggles are bookmarks and the heart means Like on Product. Correction proposed (EVAL4-PLAN.md §3A). (The Home pages used a heart for Save too; fixed in this review.)
  • "Search" is highlighted on Product: intended. The product's parent section is the catalog, which the PRD calls Search (§3D).
  • Meta lines at 12px: the small step of each scale; the muted text passes WCAG AA on every surface (5.7:1 at the lowest). Size is for you to judge, not a threshold claim.
  • Long phone scrolls: Market hall's Home is one column of eight tall cards; Editorial's sections stack as six tall tiles.

Fixed during this review

  • Market hall, phone: the search pushed the page wider than the screen (a flex basis that became a height in the narrow column layout).
  • Editorial and Bold aisle: Save used the heart icon (the app's Like); now the bookmark, like the catalog. The library's icon set gained the bookmark, and applying a concept refreshes an app's icon set that predates it (an earlier app drew the fallback sparkle).
